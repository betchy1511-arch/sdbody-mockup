Overview

What hormone replacement therapy actually is

Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) supplements hormones your body has stopped producing at the level it once did. It is one option among several, and it is only appropriate for some people.

In men, testosterone typically declines gradually from around age 30. In women the pattern is different: estrogen and progesterone fall more sharply through perimenopause and menopause, usually in the forties and fifties. For some people the shift is barely noticeable. For others it shows up as weight that will not move, workouts that stop producing results, broken sleep, low libido or a flat mood.

At SD Body, treatment starts with an evaluation rather than a prescription. A clinician reviews your symptoms, health history and labs, explains whether hormones are a likely cause, and covers the options, the risks and the monitoring plan before anything is prescribed.

Hormone decline affects both men and women, and the right response depends entirely on the individual. Some patients start hormone therapy. Others are better served by addressing sleep, training, nutrition or an underlying medical condition first.

Symptoms

Signs of a hormone imbalance

Hormones are chemical messengers produced by the glands of your endocrine system. An imbalance means levels are too high or too low for what your body needs, and because hormones regulate so much, the symptoms rarely stay in one lane.

These are the complaints we hear most often in our Mission Hills and La Jolla offices. Any one of them can have several causes, which is exactly why they are worth investigating rather than guessing at.

  • Weight gain, especially abdominal
  • Loss of muscle and strength
  • Persistent low energy
  • Trouble falling or staying asleep
  • Low sex drive
  • Erectile dysfunction
  • Low mood
  • Anxiety
  • Mood swings
  • Memory and focus problems
Causes

Why hormone levels fall

Hormone levels are not fixed for life. Several things influence how well your endocrine system works, and more than one is usually in play at once. Part of the evaluation is separating what is age, what is lifestyle and what may be a medical condition that needs treating on its own.

Age and family history

Age is the most common factor. Estrogen and progesterone fall sharply through menopause, while testosterone declines more gradually in men, sometimes beginning in the early thirties. How early and how steeply it happens is partly inherited, so we ask about family history during your first visit.

Heavy training loads

Exercise is good for you, but sustained high volume training without enough recovery or fuel can push the body to prioritise repair over other functions, including hormone production. Athletes and frequent lifters are a group we see often for exactly this reason.

Diet and chronic stress

Severe calorie restriction, very low fat intake and long-running stress all affect hormone production. Cortisol driven by chronic stress is its own factor. This is why treatment here is rarely a prescription alone, and why diet, training and sleep are part of the conversation.

Medical conditions and medications

Thyroid disease, pituitary problems, diabetes and a number of common prescriptions can all change hormone levels. Ruling these in or out is the reason evaluation comes before treatment, since the right answer may be treating the underlying condition instead.

Aging

Hormones and aging

Careful diet, consistent training and good stress management all help, but hormone production still changes with age. That change can feed the very things people blame on getting older, including lost strength, added weight around the middle and lower energy through the day.

None of that means treatment is automatic. It means the cause is worth identifying. Bring your specific symptoms to a clinician who will look at the whole picture, including labs, history and how you actually live, before recommending anything.

Who it is for

Hormone care for men and women

For men

Falling testosterone and DHEA can show up as stalled progress in the gym, added belly fat, low drive, mood changes and fatigue. Many men put those down to age and never have them looked at. An evaluation tells you whether hormones are actually the cause, and what your options are if they are. See hormone care for men.

For women

As estrogen falls through perimenopause and menopause, hot flashes, vaginal dryness, disrupted sleep, mood swings and anxiety are common. Estrogen, progesterone and in some cases testosterone are options a clinician may discuss with you, along with the risks and alternatives for each, after reviewing your history. See hormone care for women.

Diagnosis

How we work out whether HRT is right for you

The first step is an appointment. It is routine and confidential. Your clinician takes a history, asks about your symptoms and family background, and carries out a physical exam. Depending on what that suggests, laboratory testing may follow. Blood testing is the standard for most hormone assessment, and your clinician will explain which tests are being ordered and why.

Labs alone do not decide anything. A number in range does not rule out a real symptom, and a number out of range does not automatically mean hormone therapy. The result is read alongside your history, your medications and your goals.

Part of the point is ruling things out. Conditions such as thyroid disease, diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease and prostate issues can produce overlapping symptoms or change what treatment is safe. Those need to be identified before any plan is written.

Where you go for this matters. Over the counter products and one-size-fits-all online programs skip the evaluation entirely. If treatment is recommended here, you will hear the reasoning, the alternatives, the risks and how progress will be monitored.

The hormones

Hormones a clinician may discuss with you

Which of these is relevant, if any, depends on your labs, your symptoms and your history. This is background so the conversation at your appointment makes sense, not a menu to choose from.

Men and women

Testosterone

The primary male sex hormone, present in smaller amounts in women. Levels commonly decline gradually after 30, and for some people faster than that. Low levels are associated with reduced sex drive, fatigue and loss of muscle mass.

Growth hormone pathway

Sermorelin

Sermorelin acts on the pituitary gland to prompt release of your own growth hormone, rather than injecting HGH directly. It is prescribed selectively and is not FDA approved for anti-aging use, so your clinician will explain what is and is not established before it is considered.

Adrenal

DHEA

Produced in the adrenal glands, DHEA is a building block the body uses to make testosterone and estrogen. Natural levels peak in early adulthood and decline with age. A low reading on its own does not mean supplementation is needed, and any decision is made in the context of your symptoms and full results.

Metabolic

Thyroid

Thyroid hormones drive much of your metabolism. Thyroid problems mimic hormone decline closely, causing weight change, sweating, fatigue and mood swings, which is why thyroid function is checked rather than assumed.

Women

Estrogen

Central to reproductive health, and also involved in bone, liver and cardiovascular function. Levels drop sharply approaching menopause, which is when most estrogen-related symptoms appear.

Women

Progesterone

In hormone therapy, progesterone's main job is protecting the uterine lining. Women who have a uterus and take estrogen are generally prescribed progesterone alongside it to reduce the risk of endometrial overgrowth and cancer.

Benefits and risks differ by hormone, by dose and by patient. Nothing here is a recommendation for treatment.

Delivery

How hormones are prescribed

If treatment is recommended, the delivery method is chosen around the hormone, the dose you need and how you prefer to manage it. Whichever is used, regular follow-up appointments are part of the plan so levels and symptoms can be tracked and doses adjusted.

Creams and gels

Absorbed through the skin, which bypasses the digestive system. Absorption varies between people and between formulations, so this route suits some hormones and some patients better than others.

Injections

Some hormones are broken down by digestion or absorb poorly through skin, and for those an injection is the more dependable route. Given in clinic or self-administered after training.

Pills

Precisely dosed, easy to adjust and simple to stay consistent with. Oral and transdermal routes carry different risk profiles for some hormones, which is part of why the choice is made case by case.

Patches

Applied to the skin and released steadily over days. No daily pill to remember and no injection, with the patch replaced on a set schedule.

Your goals

What patients come to us wanting to change

Almost nobody arrives asking about hormone levels. They arrive because something specific has changed. These are the goals San Diego patients bring to our Mission Hills and La Jolla clinics most often, and each one is a starting point for the evaluation rather than a promised outcome.

  • Lose weight
  • Gain muscle
  • More daytime energy
  • Better sleep
  • Improved sex life
  • Erectile function
  • Low mood
  • Anxiety
  • Mood swings
  • Memory and focus

Hormones influence all of these, but so do sleep, training, nutrition, medications and other medical conditions. Where hormones are not the driver, we will tell you that. Where they are, treatment is one part of a plan rather than the whole of it. Individual results vary, and depression, anxiety and erectile dysfunction each warrant a full medical assessment. Your first visit

What to expect at your first appointment

Coming prepared makes the first appointment far more useful. It helps to bring:

  • A list of your symptoms, when they happen and how severe they are
  • Every medication and supplement you take, and how often
  • An honest picture of your diet, training and sleep
  • Any questions you want answered before deciding anything

Your clinician will carry out a physical exam and may order blood, saliva or urine testing to assess hormone levels and rule out underlying conditions. Once results are in, you and your clinician review the findings together, discuss the options including doing nothing for now, and agree on next steps and how they will be monitored.

Holistic care

Why we do not just dispense hormones

Plenty of clinics and online services will sell you a protocol without ever examining you. That is not how this works here. Hormone therapy sits alongside sleep, training, nutrition and stress, and those factors affect the same symptoms you came in about.

Your clinician sets the sequence and timing of treatment, in the clinic and outside it, and adjusts it based on how you actually respond.

Hormone therapy questions patients ask

Why do I feel off when my labs come back normal?
A lab result is one part of an evaluation. Your clinician also weighs symptoms, history, medications and exam findings. A number on its own does not explain a symptom or select a treatment.
Do you treat both men and women?
Yes. Evaluation and any treatment differ by person. See hormone care for men and for women.
Will I be on hormones forever?
Duration depends on why you started, how you respond, your risks and your preferences. Your clinician explains the monitoring plan and how treatment can be changed or stopped.
Is hormone therapy safe?
Benefits and risks vary by patient and by treatment. A qualified clinician reviews your personal and family history, contraindications, alternatives and monitoring before recommending care.
How do I get started?
Book an appointment at Mission Hills or La Jolla. The first visit is routine and confidential, and may include an exam and lab testing.
